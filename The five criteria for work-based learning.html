The five criteria for work-based learning
Criterion Description Verification and validation
1Real Work A majority of the student's responsibilities consist of substantive, career-relevant work that draws upon or extends academic learning and advances career exploration or preparation. The student submits a job description and three student-authored learning goals for approval by the relevant faculty member or other designated party.
2Meaningful Time The student completes at least 120 hours, including required training and activities integral to the role. With prior approval, two experiences may be combined to reach 120 hours, provided each independently satisfies all other criteria. Anticipated dates and hours are approved in advance. The student and supervisor confirm completion at the end; hourly logging is not required.
3Professional Supervision A qualified professional or field-specific equivalent provides appropriate training, ongoing guidance and feedback, and an end-of-experience performance evaluation. Site approval confirms the supervisor's qualifications and responsibilities. The supervisor completes an evaluation administered or supported by the Career Center.
4Expanded Network The student engages with at least two professionals beyond existing classroom, peer, and immediate supervisory relationships, gaining exposure to perspectives, pathways, communities, or opportunities relevant to their goals. Off-campus experiences are generally expected to provide these interactions through the work itself. For on-campus roles, the student and supervisor identify a plan for at least three purposeful interactions, such as informational interviews, professional meetings, external collaborations, conferences, or site visits.
5Integrated Learning The student identifies what they learned, connects it to academic and career development, and communicates the experience's value in a professional context. Through an internship course or analogous academic mechanism, the student completes structured reflection, updates their resume and professional profile, and submits Career Plan 3, connecting the experience to prior learning, strengths, development needs, and future goals.

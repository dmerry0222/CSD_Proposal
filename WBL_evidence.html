What the evidence says about work-based learning

Findings from national research on work-based learning and its effect on what happens to students after they graduate. Where a figure applies specifically to paid internships, it is labeled. Several of the strongest effects hold for work-based learning of any kind.

Compiled from Strada Education Foundation, the Center for Research on College-Workforce Transitions at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and the National Survey of College Internships. Full citations at the end.

Earnings and the quality of the first job

Effects estimated after matching students on academic and demographic characteristics
+$13,859

Higher annual personal income for bachelor's degree holders who had a work-based learning experience, compared with matched peers who did not.

Strada-Gallup Education Survey, 2022 1
+15%

Earnings one year after graduation for students who completed a paid internship, a boost of $5,564 against an average first-year salary of $36,379.

Research in Higher Education, 2026 2
+16%

The advantage does not fade. Four years out it has grown to $9,089 a year for those who held a paid internship.

Research in Higher Education, 2026 2
14 points

More likely to hold a first job that requires their degree. Students with an unpaid internship are up to six points more likely.

Research in Higher Education, 2026 2
13 points

More likely to report that their first job is part of a career rather than just a job. For a practicum, up to twelve points.

Research in Higher Education, 2026 2
14%

More likely to receive a callback for a job interview, in an audit study of employer responses to otherwise identical résumés.

Nunley et al., 2016 3

The earnings advantage for paid internships holds even after accounting for field of study, gender, and race or ethnicity. It is not simply a reflection of which students get internships in the first place.

Strada Education Foundation, 2022; Research in Higher Education, 2026 1, 2

What changes beyond pay

Work-based learning of any kind, not only paid internships
+39 points

Recent graduates with work-based learning are far more likely to say their education helped them achieve their goals. Across all degree holders, the gain is 19 points.

Strada-Gallup Education Survey, 2022 1
+11 points

More likely to agree that their education was worth the cost.

Strada-Gallup Education Survey, 2022 1
+7 points

Higher career satisfaction years after graduation.

Strada-Gallup Education Survey, 2022 1

Researchers found no statistically significant difference in the benefit of work-based learning by race, gender, or socioeconomic status, which suggests the advantage is strong across demographic groups. What differs is who gets access to the experience.

Strada Education Foundation, 2022 1

What students say the experience is worth

Students rating their own most valuable work-based learning experience
81%

Rated their experience at least 7 out of 10 on making them a stronger candidate for the job they want. Two in five rated it a perfect 10.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4
74%

Were extremely or very satisfied with their internship. Satisfaction tracks with supervisor support, mentoring, and chances to build durable skills.

National Survey of College Internships, 2023 5
8 in 10

Said a supervisor gave them quite a bit or a great deal of mentorship toward their career goals, across internships, practica, project-based learning, and undergraduate research.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4
9.3 vs 5.5

Career value, on a ten-point scale, for students whose experience expanded their professional network a great deal against those whose did not expand it at all. The single largest driver of perceived value.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4
76%

Of students in a paid internship said it helped them develop technical skills relevant to their career goals. Practica rated 81% and project-based learning 80%.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4
70%

Of interns, paid and unpaid alike, said the experience built broad skills relevant across careers, including communication, teamwork, and problem-solving.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4

Of everything researchers measured, expanding a student's professional network was the dimension most strongly linked to the career value of the experience, and paid internships delivered the strongest boost.

Strada Education Foundation, 2025 4

Students want this, and most cannot get it

The gap a shared requirement is designed to close
96%

Of students who took an internship did so to connect their education with career opportunities: to gain experience in a specific career, or to test whether a field was a good fit.

National Survey of College Internships, 2023 5
70%

Of first-year students plan to complete an internship during college.

Strada Education Foundation, 2024 5
< 1 in 4

Actually secure a paid internship. Fewer than half complete an internship of any kind.

Strada Education Foundation, 2024 5
6 in 10

Students at four-year institutions who did not intern wanted to but could not. In an earlier national sample the figure was 67%.

NSCI 2023 and 2021 5, 6
21.5%

Measured internship participation across 12,130 students, well below the 50 to 60% long assumed from earlier estimates.

National Survey of College Internships, 2021 6
55%

Of students who did not intern reported a strong interest in doing so. Interest is not the constraint.

Center for Research on College-Workforce Transitions, 2024 7

Across studies, the obstacles students name are the same and they are structural: a heavy course load is the single most-cited barrier, followed by not knowing how to find an internship, too few opportunities in their field, and the need to keep a paying job.

Strada Education Foundation, 2024; NSCI, 2021 5, 6

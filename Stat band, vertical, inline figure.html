Students arrive at Suffolk with career outcomes already at the front of their minds. They say so plainly, and they say it before they have taken a single course: the reason they enrolled is the job they expect the degree to lead to, and the help they most want from the university is help finding the work that connects to what they are studying.

That expectation is not evenly met. The students who most need structured support in turning an interest into an opportunity are frequently the ones least likely to seek it out, and the current curriculum offers them no common point of entry. A requirement changes that arithmetic, because it reaches every student rather than only the ones who already know to ask.

The figures alongside come from the entering class survey administered each summer by the Office of Institutional Research and Assessment. They are consistent year over year, and they are consistent across schools, which is part of why a shared general education requirement is the right instrument rather than a set of school-level solutions layered on top of one another.

Career Strategy & Design

Proposal for the Revised Shared Gen Ed Curriculum

Career outcomes carry substantial weight in students’ college decisions and in public scrutiny of the value of higher education. Students increasingly encounter information about graduate earnings alongside decisions about where to enroll, bringing the connection between education and opportunity into sharper institutional focus. At Suffolk, these expectations are explicit: in a local survey administered to incoming Fall 2026 first-year students during summer orientation, 93% of respondents rated obtaining a better job as a very or extremely important reason for attending college, and 84% identified developing professional skills. For students seeking greater opportunity and economic mobility, fulfilling those expectations requires intentional educational support. Students arrive with unequal access to professional networks, familiarity with workplace expectations, and knowledge of how opportunities are found and pursued. First-generation students may be navigating these expectations with less access to guidance grounded in family experience of college and professional careers. Suffolk’s educational responsibility includes helping every student develop the knowledge, relationships, experience, and judgment needed to turn that promise of opportunity into an attainable future.

Suffolk already offers substantial career expertise, committed faculty mentorship, and valuable classroom and networking experiences. Yet voluntary Career Center engagement and opportunities embedded at individual instructors’ discretion cannot ensure that every student receives coherent preparation at the right time. National research recognizes faculty’s substantial contributions to career development while identifying opportunities for stronger coordination with campus resources; student surveys also show uneven participation in career guidance. Students may encounter excellent experiences without connecting them to a developing plan, while others may miss essential preparation altogether. **We need to know what every student can do before graduation puts that preparation to the test.** Can students investigate pathways, communicate evidence of their abilities, build professional relationships, pursue meaningful experience, and act on a realistic plan? These should be visible, assessable learning outcomes throughout college.
